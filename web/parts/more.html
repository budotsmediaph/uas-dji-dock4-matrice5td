01 · Scenarios

Operating scenarios for Dock 4

DJI names six uses for Dock 4: first response, infrastructure inspection, overnight security, surveying, rescue and remote sites. In each one, the battery swap replaces a recharge between flights. The bars show airborne minutes per hour for a typical flight in each scenario, computed from DJI's charge and flight times (method in section 02).

02 · Generations

Dock 2, Dock 3 and Dock 4 compared

Figures from DJI's spec sheets for each dock and its aircraft. The biggest change is what happens between flights: Dock 2 and Dock 3 recharge their one battery inside the aircraft, while Dock 4 replaces it with a charged one. The hour view below shows what that does to flying time.

One hour at the dockRecharge the battery in the aircraft, or swap it for a charged one

20 min
  • Flying
  • On the pad: battery charging in the aircraft
  • On the pad: battery swap, landing to take-off under 45 s
  • Lower strip: charge of the battery in the aircraft, 0 to 100 %
  1. Dock 2 and Dock 3 have one battery. It stays in the aircraft. After every flight the aircraft waits on the pad while the dock recharges it: 27 min from 15 to 95 % on Dock 3, 32 min from 20 to 90 % on Dock 2.
  2. Dock 4 has four batteries. One is in the aircraft and three wait in compartments. After landing, the dock pulls the used battery out of the tail and pushes a charged one in. The swap takes under 25 s; landing to take-off, under 45 s.
  3. The used battery charges while the aircraft flies. Two charging channels each take a battery from 15 to 95 % in 27 min. In this model a charged battery is ready at every landing, at every flight length on the slider.

How the strips are worked out: a flight drains the battery at the hover rate (100 % divided by the maximum hover time, per minute). Dock 2 and Dock 3 put that charge back at DJI's published charging speed, plus 20 s for landing and closing the cover. Dock 4 spends 45 s on the pad per landing. Each strip is one hour from a long day of back-to-back flights, picked so that its flying time equals the day's average. Wind, cold and payload shorten real flights.

The screens are a generic layout made for this page. Behaviour follows DJI's published rules where they exist: no take-off above 12 m/s wind, a swap only when the aircraft is below 90 % and a stored battery is above 90 %, two batteries charging at 27 min from 15 to 95 %, at most three batteries in the compartments. Consumption, flight lengths, the weather and the scene in the camera view are invented.
